# Institutional credit memorandum examples

Research checked October 9, 2026. These are public institutional sources for presentation and analytical coverage, not evidence, credit policy or underwriting assumptions for a Zivoe deal. No source recommendations or numerical thresholds become Zivoe requirements. The six approved sections and six-page maximum remain controlling.

## Real transaction: Energy Resources USA / Bradley International Airport

[Connecticut Green Bank, November 16, 2022 Deployment Committee packet, PDF page 73 onward](https://www.ctgreenbank.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Deployment-Committee-of-the-Connecticut-Green-Bank_111622.pdf#page=73).

The November 14, 2022 credit memorandum concerns a proposed $2.5 million construction facility. It explains the financing need through the timing gap between supplier costs and payment following completion and inspection. Its transaction-structure discussion connects receivable payments, advances, capitalized interest, cash sweeps and maturity. Portions are redacted.

Application to our structure: Transaction overview should explain what the financing funds; Repayment and structure should trace when cash becomes available and what can delay or restrict it. This is more informative than simply naming receivables as collateral. Do not import the example's proposed terms or approval language.

## Real transaction: Skyview Ventures special purpose vehicle

[Connecticut Green Bank, June 25, 2021 board packet, Appendix 3, PDF page 197 onward](https://www.ctgreenbank.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Board-of-Directors-of-the-Connecticut-Green-Bank_062521_redacted.pdf#page=197).

This appendix reproduces an April 17, 2020 credit memorandum for a senior secured term loan of up to $3.5 million. The opening summarizes refinancing purpose, borrower ownership, solar-project collateral, first-priority security and forecast debt-service coverage tied to the repayment schedule. The public version substantially redacts later analysis; do not claim to have reviewed concealed financial detail.

Application: state the borrower and security perimeter precisely, distinguish asset performance from sponsor finances, and separate forecast coverage from observed performance. A collateral value does not by itself establish access to cash or enforceability. The packet includes subsequent amendments, so retain the specific memo date when discussing the example.

## Institutional format, not a completed deal: EXIM

[Export-Import Bank of the United States, Working Capital Guarantee Program credit memorandum format, revision February 2001](https://img.exim.gov/s3fs-public/forms/ebd-w-20.pdf).

The format covers the request, ownership and operations, working-capital needs, collateral and proposed advance rates, management, credit history, financial-statement quality, financial analysis and guarantors. Its financial-analysis guidance asks the writer to explain changes and trends rather than merely repeat balances.

Application: record whether statements are management-prepared, compiled, reviewed or audited, including qualifications; explain material liquidity, leverage and earnings movements; evaluate guarantor support separately from borrower resources. This is an older institutional format, not evidence of current EXIM rules and not a completed credit memo. Its regulatory provisions, ratios and advance rates are not adopted.

## Additional lead with limited access

[Asian Development Bank, SK Finance project 58140-001](https://www.adb.org/projects/58140-001/main) lists an August 2024 Report and Recommendation of the President and a senior secured financing of up to $100 million. The project page was accessible; the full report download was not. It is a useful lead for a financial-intermediary transaction, but no analytical conclusions here rely on an unread report.

## Incorporation into the six sections

1. Summary: transaction, key measured results and material unresolved evidence. No recommendation.
2. Transaction overview: compact terms; sourced financing purpose and sources/uses when relevant.
3. Collateral and portfolio analysis: composition, performance, concentrations and eligibility, with explicit dates, population and definitions.
4. Borrower, originator and sponsor: roles, financial condition, liquidity, statement quality, servicing and separately identified guarantor resources.
5. Repayment and structure: cash timing and availability, contractual payment order, security perimeter and practical dependencies; distinguish forecasts from observations.
6. Sources and review scope: dated source versions, assurance status, gaps, reconciliations and estimates.

These are editorial applications to Zivoe's structure, not a claim that every institution uses these exact six headings. The reviewed real-deal memoranda are from one public lender; this is not a representative survey of private-bank credit committees. Redactions and public disclosure limit what can be assessed. Retain detailed calculations and evidence records separately while keeping material caveats in the memo.
